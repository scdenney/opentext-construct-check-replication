## Wave 3 search (17 Nov 2026): inclusion rule unchanged; search net wider than the rule

Decided on 17 Nov, before any wave-3 answer was read and before any wave-3 estimate existed.

The brief given to the wave-3 searchers (`revision/wave3/BRIEF.md`) asked them to report any public dataset with a randomized treatment before an open-text answer and roughly 150 or more answers, and to flag, not require, an author-stated expectation. That net is wider than the inclusion rule on purpose: a search should not decide inclusion. Inclusion is decided at triage (`revision/wave3/TRIAGE.md`) with the four criteria printed in the SI, unchanged from waves 1 and 2:

1. the text is linked to a randomized treatment at the respondent or task level;
2. the original authors state which consideration the treatment should raise (a hypothesis or a stated mechanism; they need not have analyzed the text, as with Lupu & Wallace 2024 in wave 2);
3. the licence allows reuse;
4. at least 300 non-empty, non-excluded answers. The SI said "about 300"; for wave 3 the number is a floor.

The rule is not relaxed to admit smaller wave-3 studies, because the results for the first 53 studies are known and a rule changed now could be chosen with them in view. Every candidate that reached a download or a card and failed a criterion is listed in `TRIAGE.md` with the reason.

One study already in the corpus does not meet criteria 2 and 4: `hartnett2022_whytrump` (181 answers; the authors expect no effect). It was registered in wave 1 as a null-expected check and is kept; the SI now says so.

**Result of the labeler check (17 Nov 2026).** The Qwen3.6-35B labeling run finished on 29 Oct (job 5364294). It was frozen in its own locks (`<id>.stmq.k1.json`) and estimated by `rv_13c_stm_labeler_check.R`; the primary STM arm is unchanged. The two labelers choose the same topic for 54 of 83 targets; primary contrasts are significant in 30% (Qwen) against 29% (Gemma) and have the theorized sign in 52% against 56%.

## Criterion (iii) applied consistently: Sambanis & Lee 2025 is included (17 Nov 2026)

In wave 2, `sambanis2025_externalthreat` was excluded because its archive "has no licence and asks users to make contact before use". The wording in question is Harvard Dataverse's default text for a dataset without a chosen licence ("This dataset is made available with limited information on how it can be used. You may wish to communicate with the Contact(s) specified before use."). `bisgaard2019_econnews` carries exactly the same text and was included in wave 1 with three cards. The two decisions cannot both stand.

The rule is now stated once and applied to every wave: criterion (iii) is met by an explicit open licence, or by a public replication archive of a published article under the repository's default terms with no stated restriction. It is not met when a restriction is stated (access "may be restricted or reviewed": Wang 2026), when the material is not a public deposit (a view-only review link: Blankenship 2023), or when there is neither a licence nor a publication (Murphy 2026).

Under this rule Bisgaard stays, Sambanis & Lee is included, and Sperber et al. 2024 (wave 3, same default text) is included. Sambanis & Lee's card was drafted blind on 28 Oct and had never been registered or run, so the decision was made without any result for that study. The alternative, removing the three Bisgaard cards, would have changed the corpus after its results were known.

## STM arm: the estimation fold was projected with the wrong word numbers (found and corrected 19 Nov 2026)

This is a coding error, found after the estimation folds had been read, and its correction changes results.

- **The error.** Topic proportions for estimation-fold answers come from `stm::fitNewDocuments()`, which needs each answer's words numbered as in the fitted model's vocabulary. The scripts built those numbers with `quanteda::convert(to = "stm")`. That function drops every word with no occurrence in the answers it is given and renumbers the rest. Whenever one or more words of the discovery-fold vocabulary did not occur in the estimation fold, every word after the first absent one was read as a different word. This was the case in 128 of 139 studies; in the median study 88 percent of estimation-fold tokens came after the first absent word. The projected proportion of the chosen topic correlated with the correct one at a median of 0.16.
- **How it was found.** An agent porting the topic-model step into the `opencheck` package (19 Nov) compared the reference code with a version that maps words by name and reported the difference. It was then confirmed on all studies.
- **What was affected.** Every quantity that uses the projected estimation fold: the topic-model arm (`rv_13_stm_arm.R`, measure step), its diagnostics (`rv_13b`, `rv_13c`), the robustness checks over the number of topics (`rv_13e`), and the split procedure of the double-dipping simulation (`rv_08_doubledip.R`). Not affected: the models themselves (fit on the discovery fold), the choice of topic (made from discovery-fold words and examples), the stability of the number of topics (`rv_13d`), the full-sample procedure of the simulation, and every language-model arm.
- **The correction.** `stm_documents()` in `rv_config.R` numbers the words by their position in the model's vocabulary; the five scripts use it. Nothing else changed: the frozen models, the frozen topic choices and the locks are the ones written before. The old measures and tables are kept in `data/results/set_aside/stm_projection_bug/`.
- **What changed in the results.** The topic-model arm recovers 41 percent of 182 primary contrasts (it was reported as 23 percent), and 67 percent of its estimates have the theorized sign (58 before). The chosen topic is the best-correlated one for 50 percent of targets (24 before), and the best topic in hindsight correlates with the yes/no label at a median of 0.27 (0.18 before). For the naturalization study, the pooled model's chosen topic now moves in the theorized direction. The yes/no, rating, induced-codebook and pairwise results are unchanged.
- **Double-dipping simulation.** The split procedure was rerun for all studies with the corrected projection (`rv_08_rerun_split.sh`, finished 19 Nov 15:02; the full-sample columns were carried over). The mean split rate over 138 studies is 0.049 with the selected topic (0.050 before the correction; range 0.010 to 0.085) and 0.050 with a fixed topic (0.049 before). Under permuted labels any fixed function of the text is unrelated to the permuted treatment, so the old split rates were valid as rates for a split procedure, but they were rates for a garbled measure and are replaced. The naturalization study's figures did not change.


## STM arm with the treatment covariate (planned 22 Nov 2026, before any result of it)

This is an added robustness check, decided after the main results were known.

- **Why.** The topic-model arm fits STM on the discovery fold without a covariate, so the contrast under test cannot shape the topics. STM's distinctive feature is to let prevalence depend on covariates such as treatment, and the paper's draft claim that topic models, the structural topic model included, are inadequate as the construct check was stronger than that test supports. A side agent reading the session pointed this out before submission (an earlier wording of this entry said "a reviewer"; corrected 23 Nov 2026).
- **What changes, and only that.** `STM_VARIANT=cov` in `rv_13_stm_arm.R`: the discovery-fold fit adds the randomized arms as a prevalence covariate (`prevalence = ~arm`, the joint value of the study's contrasts from `rv_treatments.R`), at the K the original fit chose for that study. Tokenization, vocabulary, initialization (spectral), iterations (75), seed (42), the labeler (Gemma 4 12B, FP8, L4, same prompt), the topic-choice rule and the effect estimator are unchanged. The two original-paper country fits of the naturalization study are not repeated.
- **What stays covariate-free.** The estimation fold is projected with `fitNewDocuments(prevalence = NULL)` (average prior), as in the original arm, so treatment never enters the measure that is tested.
- **Separate artifacts.** Models `data/jobs/stm/<id>.stmcov.rds`, labeling requests `label_requests_cov.jsonl` and run `stm_labelcov_gemma4_12b`, topic choices `<id>.stmcov.topic_choice.csv`, locks `revision/locks/<id>.stmcov.k1.json`, measures `data/results/a1/<id>/stmcov/measures.csv` (model key `stmcov`, arm D_topic). The original arm's files are not touched.
- **Environment.** R 4.6.1 with stm 1.3.8 and quanteda 4.3.1 reinstalled at the versions the original fits used (R was upgraded from 4.5 after those fits); Korean tokens from kiwipiepy 0.22.2 under Python 3.14 (`KIWI_PYTHON`).
- **Reporting.** Detection on the primary contrasts is reported for both STM arms side by side, whatever the result, and the paper's topic-model claim is written from it.
- **Results (22 Nov 2026, after the run).** Fits: all 139 discovery samples, K as in the original fit for every study, vocabulary identical in 133 (the five Korean studies and `nwo26_exp2` differ slightly, Jaccard >= 0.974, from the reinstalled tokenizer environment). Labels: `stm_labelcov_gemma4_12b` (job 5703135, 203 targets, 185 matched against 181 for the original arm). Measures under new locks `<id>.stmcov.k1.json`; effects in `analysis/output/revision/effects_stmcov.csv` (`ONLY_MODELS=stmcov` in `rv_06_effects.R`, same estimator, 2,000 permutations, seed 42). Against the original arm on the 205 primary contrasts: detection 39 percent (36), 42 percent of the 188 covered contrasts (41 percent of 182), theorized sign 74 percent (66), smaller arm under 200 responses 28 percent (17), human-coded contrasts 20 of 46 (16), median AUC against the hand codes 0.63 (0.64). The zero-shot yes/no prompt with the reference model detects 67 percent. Comparison script and report: `analysis/revision/rv_21_stm_vs_llm.py`, `analysis/output/revision/stm_vs_llm.md`.

## Topic-model refits in one environment (23 Nov 2026, written before any refit result)

Decided after the reproducibility check above, with all main results known. Script `analysis/revision/rv_26_stm_refits.R`.

- **What.** Both topic-model arms (no covariate; `prevalence = ~arm`) are refit on the discovery fold 20 times per study, on one machine (Mac, R 4.6.1, stm 1.3.8, quanteda 4.3.1, one BLAS thread, kiwipiepy 0.22.2 under Python 3.14). K is the frozen K; spectral initialization, 75 iterations, seed 42, tokenization, topic labeler (Gemma 4 12B, FP8, L4, same prompt), topic-choice rule and effect estimator (2,000 permutations, seed 42) are those of `rv_13` and `rv_06`.
- **What varies.** Only the order of documents and of vocabulary columns, which carries no information about the data. Spectral initialization is deterministic, so changing the seed would not give a different start. Refit 0 uses the file order of documents and the vocabulary sorted by byte order (independent of locale); refits 1 to 19 are random orders (seed 20261123 + refit). Both arms use the same order in a given refit, so the covariate comparison is paired and within one environment. `quanteda::convert(to = "stm")` sorts the vocabulary by locale and is not used here.
- **Not varied.** K, preprocessing, the labeler, the split. The range understates what a researcher free to vary those would see. Other initializations (random, LDA) are not tried.

The frozen fits are listed beside the refits. If the frozen figures fall outside the range, the refit median replaces them in the note.
- **Example (Figure panel c).** The SK/TW example of `rv_08` is refit in 20 orders (full sample and split). Some such refits were already seen on 23 Nov (see above), so this part is not blind. The estimation fold is read again.
- **Locks.** Fits and topic choices are frozen per study (`<id>.stmrefit.k1.json`) before the estimation fold is opened. Outputs go to `data/jobs/stm_refit/` and `data/results/stm_refit/`, so nothing under `data/results/a1` changes.
